Welcome to Xi'an

The eternal capital, the start of the Silk Road

Known through history as Chang'an, "the City of Eternal Peace," Xi'an is China's most storied ancient capital and the eastern starting point of the Silk Road. For over 3,100 years and 13 dynasties, this was the seat of imperial power — and today it's a rare place where an intact Ming-era city wall, grand Tang pagodas, Muslim courtyard quarters, and sleek modern waterfront districts all sit within the same city limits.

Beyond the world-famous Terracotta Warriors, Xi'an's real charm is in how royal grandeur and everyday street life sit side by side. Cycle the full 13.7 km ancient wall at sunset, wander lantern-lit Muslim Quarter alleys thick with the smell of noodles and lamb, and watch the fountains dance under the Big Wild Goose Pagoda after dark.

⚱️

Imperial Archaeology

Home to the Terracotta Army and thousands of years of dynastic relics — few places on earth offer this depth of ancient Chinese history in one destination.

🐫

Silk Road Heritage

Chang'an was the eastern terminus of the Silk Road — a melting pot of Persian, Central Asian, and Buddhist culture whose traces remain in the Muslim Quarter and Great Mosque today.

🏮

Tang Dynasty Nightscapes

Qujiang New District glows after dark with lantern-lit streets, musical fountains, and immersive Tang-style performances recreating the golden age of Chang'an.

🍜

Northern Shaanxi Cuisine

Hand-torn biangbiang noodles, lamb soup with crumbled flatbread, and roujiamo — bold, hearty wheat-and-lamb cooking rooted in Guanzhong farming and Silk Road fusion.

History & Architecture

13 dynasties, one continuous timeline

Before the Qin Dynasty, Xi'an was already the heart of Guanzhong civilization. In 221 BC Emperor Qin Shi Huang unified China here and built his mausoleum guarded by the Terracotta Army. Under the Han Dynasty, Chang'an became the eastern anchor of the Silk Road, drawing merchants, monks, and diplomats from Persia and Central Asia.

The Tang Dynasty (618–907) was Chang'an's golden age — briefly the largest city on earth, with over a million residents, grand palaces, and flourishing Buddhism. After the Ming Dynasty rebuilt the city with the massive brick wall that still encircles the old town, Xi'an settled into its role as a cradle of Chinese civilization, one that today links inland China to Eurasian trade once again.

🏯

Ming City Wall & Bell-Drum Towers

China's best-preserved complete city fortification — 13.7 km of 12-metre brick ramparts. At the historic centre, the symmetrical Bell and Drum Towers once kept time for the whole city.

🛕

Tang Buddhist Pagodas

Simple, solid brick towers built to shelter Silk Road scriptures. The Big Wild Goose Pagoda and its slender sibling, the Small Wild Goose Pagoda, are the finest surviving examples.

🕌

Sino-Islamic Courtyards

The Great Mosque hides deep in the Muslim Quarter — Chinese courtyard layouts and upturned-eave wooden halls carrying Islamic geometric carving, with no domes or minarets in sight.

⚱️

Underground Qin Mausoleum

Beneath Lishan lies one of the greatest archaeological finds in history — thousands of individually sculpted Terracotta Warriors, each with unique facial features.

Areas & Sights

Where to go and what to see in each district

Xi'an's essential sights cluster into a handful of distinct zones — the walled old city, the Tang-heritage new district to the south, the Muslim Quarter's back lanes, and the Terracotta Army complex out toward Lishan. Most visitors can cover the old town on foot and reach everything else by metro or a short taxi ride.

Old City Center 老城区 Iconic · Start Here

The historic heart within the Ming wall — Bell Tower, Drum Tower, the full circuit of city ramparts, and the Muslim Quarter's food-stall alleys all within walking distance of each other. Best explored as a full day, combining daytime architecture with an evening food crawl.

The 7-story brick pagoda commissioned by Monk Xuanzang, a UNESCO Silk Road site. Reserve time in the north square for Asia's largest musical fountain show after dark.

Great Mosque & Muslim Quarter Back Alleys 清真大寺 Hidden Gem

Duck off the main food street and into the Great Mosque's quiet multi-courtyard complex, then keep wandering the residential back alleys beyond it — fewer crowds, more authentic halal snacks, and a completely different pace than the main thoroughfare.

Great Mosque 清真大寺
A unique fusion of Chinese courtyard architecture and Islamic carving, built across Ming-Qing era courtyards. Modest clothing required; visitors may not enter prayer halls.
¥25 (¥15 winter) 8:00–18:00
Residential Back Lanes 回坊小巷
Quiet courtyard residences and small halal eateries a few streets back from the main tourist strip — the cheapest, most authentic food in the city.
Free Open 24h
Daming Palace Heritage Park 大明宫国家遗址公园 Tang Imperial Ruins

The vast foundation ruins of the Tang Dynasty's grandest imperial palace. Open grounds and miniature models reveal just how enormous ancient Chang'an's royal court once was — a peaceful, uncrowded sunset walk.

Daming Palace National Heritage Park 大明宫国家遗址公园
Open archaeological grounds with palace foundation relics and scale models of Tang Chang'an. A relaxed contrast to the busier sights in town.
¥60 9:30–18:00
Lishan & Terracotta Army Scenic Zone 骊山 · 兵马俑 Day Trip · Book Ahead

About an hour east of downtown — the single most famous archaeological site in China, alongside the Tang imperial hot spring palace at its foot. Allocate at least half a day, and reserve tickets in advance.

Getting There

Airport, high-speed rail, and reaching the city centre

From Xianyang International Airport (XIY)

  • Metro Line 14 — cheapest, most reliableDirect metro connection into the city centre, roughly 40–50 minutes.
  • Airport shuttle bus — several city-centre routesRuns to major hotels and the railway station area; slower than the metro but simple with luggage.
  • DiDi or taxi — most convenientRoughly 45–60 minutes to the old town depending on traffic. Best option late at night.

By High-Speed Rail

  • Beijing → Xi'an — around 4.5 to 6 hoursOne of the busiest domestic HSR corridors; book on Trip.com or the 12306 app.
  • Chengdu → Xi'an — around 3.5 to 4 hoursCrosses the scenic Qinling mountain range.
  • Xi'an North Railway StationThe main HSR hub, connected to the metro and a fair distance from the old city centre — budget extra time.

Getting Around

Metro, tourist buses, and when to just walk

Xi'an's metro network covers downtown relics, Qujiang District, and — via Line 9 — the Terracotta Army. English signage is available throughout. The old city wall, Muslim Quarter, and Bell Tower zones are best explored on foot.

Day Trips

Easy escapes from the city centre

Terracotta Army & Lishan 兵马俑

🚌 ~1 hrHalf to full day

China's most famous archaeological site. Advance online reservation mandatory — tickets sell out fast on weekends and holidays. Combine with Huaqing Palace for a full day.

Huaqing Palace 华清宫

🚌 ~1 hrHalf day

Tang imperial hot spring palace at the foot of Lishan, with mountain and lake views — easily paired with the Terracotta Army.

Mount Hua 华山

🚄 ~1 hrFull day

One of China's most dramatic mountains, famous for its narrow plank walkways and sheer cliffside paths. A serious day out — start early and check cable car options in advance.

All popular sites here require online reservation 3–7 days in advance. Tickets sell out quickly on weekends and Chinese public holidays.
