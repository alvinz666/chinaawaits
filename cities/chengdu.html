Welcome to Chengdu

The City of Hibiscus, the Land of Abundance

Known through history as Rongcheng, "the City of Hibiscus," and Tianfu Zhi Guo, "the Land of Abundance," Chengdu is the capital of Sichuan Province, set on a fertile plain ringed by mountains. Unlike China's fast-paced megacities, it balances millennia of ancient Shu civilisation, poetic Tang-dynasty heritage, timeless teahouse culture, and sleek modern commercial districts into a uniquely relaxed urban rhythm.

For over 2,300 years Chengdu has been the cultural, culinary, and creative heart of western China — isolated enough by surrounding mountains to develop its own self-sufficient, unhurried way of life. Its real magic is in the small pleasures: watching pandas munch bamboo at dawn, sipping covered-bowl tea under old banyan trees, and savouring numbing-spicy street snacks on a slow afternoon.

History & Architecture

From Bronze-Age Shu to Three Kingdoms capital

Chengdu is one of China's oldest continuously inhabited cities, its history reaching back to the Bronze-Age Shu Kingdom. The mysterious civilisation unearthed at the Jinsha and Sanxingdui sites reveals an advanced culture entirely separate from China's central plains.

During the Three Kingdoms era, Chengdu became the capital of Shu-Han, leaving deep heritage tied to Zhuge Liang and Liu Bei — and the poet Du Fu settled here to write his most famous verses. Centuries of mild climate and fertile plains brought steady prosperity, fostering the relaxed, pleasure-oriented lifestyle that still defines the city — now layered with booming creative industries and youth culture.

🏯

Sichuan Classical Courtyard & Garden

Low-rise wooden courtyard houses with grey tile roofs and interior ponds to dispel summer heat. Wuhou Shrine, Du Fu Thatched Cottage, and Wenshu Monastery are the finest examples.

🏮

Old Chengdu Alley Courtyards

Kuanzhai Alleys preserve the wide, narrow, and well-lane layout of late Qing Chengdu — grey brick courtyard homes now mixing teahouses, craft shops, and boutique inns.

🏙️

Blended Ancient & Modern

Taikoo Li integrates the ancient wooden halls and pagodas of Daci Temple with minimalist glass-and-stone retail — one of Chengdu's most iconic architectural contrasts.

🎨

Industrial Retro Creative

Eastern Suburbs Memory repurposes an old factory complex — chimneys, red brick workshops, and machinery turned into graffiti walls, galleries, and live music venues.

Areas & Sights

Where to go and what to see in each district

Chengdu's essentials spread across a compact, metro-connected footprint — pandas to the north, ancient temples and alleys around the old centre, and a retro creative district to the east. Most visitors can comfortably cover the core sights in three unhurried days.

Panda Base & Northern Suburbs 熊猫基地 Family · Arrive Early

Chengdu's single most famous attraction, and rightly so. Pandas are most active in the cool of early morning — arrive before 7:30am if you want to see them moving rather than napping in the shade.

Kuanzhai Alleys 宽窄巷子 Old Chengdu · Teahouses

Three parallel lanes — wide, narrow, and well — lined with grey-brick courtyard homes from the late Qing dynasty. Skip the crowded main lane and duck into the quiet side alleys for the more authentic atmosphere.

Kuanzhai Alleys 宽窄巷子
Wander the old courtyards and sit down for covered-bowl tea in a traditional teahouse — the whole point here is to slow down, not to check off sights.
Free Stores 10:00–22:00
Wuhou Shrine & Jinli Ancient Street 武侯祠 · 锦里 Three Kingdoms · Night Market

Chengdu's deepest Three Kingdoms heritage sits right beside its most atmospheric night market. Visit Wuhou Shrine in daylight, then let Jinli's red lanterns carry you into the evening.

Wuhou Shrine 武侯祠
A Ming-Qing temple complex with red walls, ancient cypress trees, and symmetrical ancestral halls — the top landmark of Three Kingdoms architecture, centred on Zhuge Liang.
¥50 8:00–18:30
Jinli Ancient Street 锦里
A restored Ming-Qing commercial arcade draped with red lanterns, recreating a historic night market. Come back after 6pm once the lanterns are lit.
Free Shops 9:00–22:30
Qintai Ancient Path & Sichuan Opera 琴台路
Book an evening at Shufeng Yayun Theatre for face-changing (bian lian), fire-spitting, and hand-shadow puppetry — Chengdu's signature folk art.
Varies Evening shows

Getting There

Two airports, high-speed rail, and reaching the city centre

Chengdu has two airports — Shuangliu (CTU), closer to downtown, and Tianfu (TFU), further out and handling a growing share of flights. Check carefully which one your flight uses.

From Shuangliu Airport (CTU)

  • Metro — cheapest, most reliableDirect metro line into the city centre, roughly 30–40 minutes.
  • DiDi or taxi — most convenientRoughly 30–50 minutes to downtown depending on traffic. Best option late at night.

From Tianfu Airport (TFU)

  • Metro / intercity railConnected to the city centre, though the journey runs closer to an hour given the distance.
  • Airport shuttle busRuns to major downtown hotels and transport hubs — simpler with heavy luggage.

By High-Speed Rail

  • Xi'an → Chengdu — around 3.5 to 4 hoursCrosses the scenic Qinling mountain range.
  • Xipu Station — the gateway to nearby heritage sitesReaches Dujiangyan and Qingcheng Mountain in around 30 minutes — ideal for a day trip.

Getting Around

Metro, shared bikes, and when to just walk

Chengdu's metro network covers nearly all downtown attractions with English signage throughout, and the city's flat geography makes it one of the best in China for shared bikes. Most of the historic core — Kuanzhai, Wuhou Shrine, Wenshu — is comfortably walkable once you're there.

Day Trips

World heritage sites and ancient civilisation, all reachable in a day

⛰️
Dujiangyan Irrigation System, at the foot of Qingcheng Mountain

Dujiangyan & Qingcheng Mountain 都江堰 · 青城山

🚄 ~30 minFull day

A 2,200-year-old dam-free irrigation system, still functioning today, paired with Taoist cliff temples on a forested mountain nearby. Reach both via Xipu Station.

Sanxingdui Museum 三星堆博物馆

🚄 ~1 hrFull day

Startling bronze masks and artifacts from the mysterious Bronze-Age Shu civilisation — one of China's most unusual archaeological finds, distinct from anything in the central plains.

Leshan Giant Buddha 乐山大佛

🚄 ~1–1.5 hrFull day

The world's largest stone-carved Buddha, seated at the confluence of three rivers. A longer day trip but a worthwhile one — arrive early to beat the queues for the cliffside stairway.

Most museums close on Mondays — reserve tickets online in advance, especially during Chinese public holidays.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from first-time Chengdu visitors

Three days covers the pandas, the old town temples and alleys, and the poetic gardens without rushing. Five days lets you add the Sanxingdui and Jinsha museums plus a full-day trip out to Dujiangyan and Qingcheng Mountain.
As early as possible — ideally before 7:30am, and no later than 10am. Pandas are most active in the cool morning hours and tend to nap through the afternoon, especially in warmer months.
Most Sichuan cooking defaults to spicy, and the numbing peppercorn (málà) sensation is a signature flavour, not an optional extra. If you'd rather avoid it, say "Bu Yao La" (no chilli) when ordering — most restaurants will accommodate.
Shuangliu (CTU) is closer to downtown and handles the bulk of flights; Tianfu (TFU) is newer and further out. Check your ticket carefully — the two are a fair distance apart.
Yes, if you want to use Google, Instagram, WhatsApp, Gmail, or most Western apps — all blocked in mainland China. Download and test a VPN before arriving; it cannot be installed once you're inside the country. See the internet guide for recommended options.
Most museums, including Jinsha Site Museum, close on Mondays. Reserve tickets online in advance, particularly around Chinese public holidays when popular sites sell out.
Very safe by international standards. The usual sensible precautions apply — watch for pickpockets in crowded night markets, and be aware of tourist-targeted scams around major attractions.
