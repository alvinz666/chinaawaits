China's innovation capital — a city that went from fishing village to tech megacity in 40 years, with a coastline, world-class Cantonese food, and a creative scene that keeps surprising.

Welcome to Shenzhen

The world's fastest city, still accelerating

No city on earth has grown faster than Shenzhen. In 1980 it was a collection of Cantonese fishing villages with a population of around 30,000. Deng Xiaoping chose it as China's first Special Economic Zone, and the results were unprecedented: today it is home to 18 million people, to global technology companies like Huawei, Tencent, and DJI, and to one of the most dynamic urban cultures in Asia.

For foreign visitors, Shenzhen offers something few major Chinese cities can match: immediacy. It sits minutes from Hong Kong across a land border, making it the easiest introduction to mainland China for many travellers. But it rewards those who dig deeper — into the creative districts, the ancient walled settlements at its edges, and a food scene that draws on the full depth of Cantonese and Hakka tradition.

⚡

Tech & Innovation

Home to Huawei, Tencent, DJI, and thousands of startups. Shenzhen manufactures more hardware and produces more patents per capita than almost any city in the world.

🎨

Art & Design

A UNESCO Creative City of Design. OCT Loft, Dafen Oil Painting Village, and OCAT anchor a thriving independent art and design scene built into repurposed industrial spaces.

🌊

Coast & Green Space

15 km of Shenzhen Bay promenade, sandy beaches at Dameisha, and more green space per capita than most Chinese cities. Wutong Mountain offers a genuine wilderness hike within city limits.

🇭🇰

Gateway from Hong Kong

The easiest major mainland Chinese city to reach from Hong Kong — a 15-minute walk across the border. Also highly accessible by high-speed rail from Guangzhou, and internationally by Bao'an Airport.

History & Identity

Four decades that changed a nation

Before 1980, Shenzhen was a cluster of Cantonese and Hakka fishing villages. Nantou Ancient City — now surrounded by modern Nanshan — had been a walled settlement since the Eastern Jin dynasty in 317 AD, serving as the administrative centre for the entire Pearl River Delta region for over a thousand years. But the land around it was rice paddies and coast.

When Deng Xiaoping designated Shenzhen as China's first Special Economic Zone, it became a laboratory for controlled capitalism. Foreign investment arrived, factories rose overnight, and migrants from across China flooded in. The phrase "Shenzhen speed" entered the language: a new skyscraper floor every three days at the height of the construction boom. Today a bronze statue of Deng stands at the summit of Lianhuashan Park, looking out over the skyline he conjured into existence.

Ancient Roots

Nantou Ancient City (南头古城) has stood since 317 AD — a 1,700-year-old walled settlement now encircled by apartment towers in Nanshan, still accessible on foot.

🏗️

The SEZ Miracle

Designated China's first Special Economic Zone in 1980. Within a decade it had become one of the fastest-growing cities in human history, with a population doubling every few years.

🔌

Electronics Capital

Huaqiangbei became the world's largest electronics market during the 1990s–2000s manufacturing boom. It remains the best place on earth to buy components, gadgets, and hardware.

🚀

Innovation Hub

Huawei, Tencent, and DJI all call Shenzhen home. The city now files more international patents annually than most countries, and is developing rapidly as a centre for biotech and AI.

Areas & Sights

Where to go and what to see in each district

Shenzhen's main districts stretch west to east along the coast, with Futian (the CBD) at the centre. Nanshan and Shekou sit to the west; Luohu and the Hong Kong border to the east; Dapeng Peninsula to the far east. The metro connects nearly all of them.

Futian 福田 CBD · Start Here

Shenzhen's commercial and civic heart. The Ping An Finance Centre — China's second-tallest building — dominates the skyline. Lianhuashan Park sits at the centre, offering the best free views in the city. For visitors, Futian is the most practical base: excellent metro connections, a wide choice of hotels, and the busiest concentration of restaurants, malls, and nightlife.

Ping An Finance Centre 平安国际金融中心
China's second-tallest building at 599 m. The observation deck on floor 116 gives a full 360° view across the city, Shenzhen Bay, and into Hong Kong's New Territories on clear days.
¥280 10:00–22:00
Lianhuashan Park 莲花山公园
A broad hilltop park in the heart of Futian. Locals do tai chi here at dawn; the summit plateau holds a large bronze statue of Deng Xiaoping and the best free panorama of the CBD. 15–30 min walk to the top on shaded paths.
Free 6:00–22:00
Shenzhen Museum 深圳博物馆
Permanent exhibitions on the city's history from ancient Baiyue culture through the SEZ era. One of the more rewarding free museums in Guangdong — well-presented in English and Chinese.
Free Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00
Coco Park & MixC 购物区
Futian's main shopping and nightlife zones. Coco Park has an open-air bar and restaurant strip that stays lively late. MixC is larger and more upscale. Both are walkable from the CBD metro stations.
Free entry 10:00–22:00+
Nanshan & Shekou 南山 · 蛇口 Tech · Waterfront · Expat

Nanshan is where Tencent's global headquarters sits alongside Shenzhen Bay, OCT Harbour, and the city's most liveable cycling path. Shekou, at the southern tip of the peninsula, is Shenzhen's most international neighbourhood — shaped by expats, maritime history, and a waterfront dining scene anchored by Sea World Plaza, built around a permanently docked cruise ship.

Shenzhen Bay Park 深圳湾公园
A 15 km coastal promenade running the full length of Shenzhen Bay. Free, open, and serene — locals cycle, skate, and walk dogs here daily. On clear days you can see the hills of Hong Kong's New Territories across the water. Best at golden hour.
Free Open 24h
OCT Harbour 欢乐港湾
A large waterfront development on Qianhai Bay with an aquarium, restaurants, a giant Ferris wheel, and a free nightly water and LED light show. Best visited in the evening — the light show runs around 8pm and is genuinely impressive.
Free (light show) / ¥160 (aquarium) Light show ~20:00 nightly
Sea World Plaza, Shekou 海上世界
Shenzhen's most expat-friendly nightlife zone — dining and bar streets surrounding a permanently docked cruise ship converted into a hotel. German beer halls, Japanese izakayas, Mexican cantinas, and live music venues. Relaxed and genuinely international in feel.
Free to enter Best after 18:00
Nantou Ancient City 南头古城
A 1,700-year-old walled settlement now encircled by apartment towers. Walk through the ancient gate into a neighbourhood of old temples, heritage buildings, galleries, and independent cafés. One of Shenzhen's most atmospheric and underrated spots.
Free Open daily
DJI Sky City & Flagship Store 大疆天空之城
The global drone leader's dramatic new headquarters in Nanshan — an architectural landmark by Foster + Partners, with two towers connected by a sky bridge. The DJI flagship store nearby lets you see and try the full product range. Worth a detour for tech enthusiasts.
Store: Free 10:00–21:00
Enshang Wetland Park 恩上湿地公园
A serene wetland nature reserve tucked into the hills of northern Nanshan — boardwalks over still water, herons, lotus ponds, and almost no tourists. A genuine antidote to Shenzhen's relentless urbanism. Best visited on a weekday morning.
Free Open daily

OCT Loft & Overseas Chinese Town 华侨城创意文化园 Art · Design · Culture

OCT Loft is Shenzhen's creative quarter — a former industrial compound redeveloped into galleries, independent bookshops, design studios, and some of the city's best independent coffee. The wider OCT district also contains the family-oriented theme park cluster (Happy Valley, Window of the World) and OCAT, one of the most ambitious contemporary art institutions in southern China.

OCT Loft Creative District 华侨城创意文化园
Wide brick courtyards, ivy-covered warehouse galleries, indie design shops, and a dense concentration of specialty coffee — Shenzhen's answer to Beijing's 798, but quieter and arguably more interesting. Free to walk around; most spaces open from 10am.
Free 10:00–21:00
OCAT Shenzhen 华·美术馆
The district's flagship contemporary art institution with ambitious rotating exhibitions. Programming tends toward international artists and large-scale installations. Consistently one of the better contemporary art spaces in Guangdong.
¥20–50 Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00
Window of the World 世界之窗
130 scaled-down global landmarks — Eiffel Tower, Pyramids, Taj Mahal, Niagara Falls — crammed into a theme park. Extremely kitsch, but huge fun for the right mood. The nightly cultural performance shows are genuinely entertaining.
¥230 10:00–22:00
Zhongshuge Bookstore 钟书阁书店
One of China's most visually spectacular bookshops — a theatrical space of floor-to-ceiling mirrored shelves, dramatic lighting, and carefully curated books. The Shenzhen branch is a design landmark in its own right. Even if you don't read Chinese, it's worth seeing.
Free 10:00–22:00
📚
Zhongshuge Bookstore — one of China's most theatrical book spaces
Luohu & Huaqiangbei 罗湖 · 华强北 Shopping · HK Gateway

Luohu is Shenzhen's historic heart — the oldest commercial district, abutting the Hong Kong border at Luohu crossing. Huaqiangbei, just north, is the world's most extraordinary electronics market: towers of components, drones, phone parts, vintage game consoles, and anything else with a circuit board. Even if you're not buying, an hour here is one of the most memorable walks in China.

Huaqiangbei Electronics Market 华强北电子市场
Multiple interconnected malls — SEG Plaza, Mingtong, Saige — each packed with hundreds of stalls selling components, gadgets, drones, retro games, cables, and custom tech. The scale and variety is genuinely astonishing. Best explored on foot between buildings over 1–2 hrs.
Free entry ~10:00–19:00
Dongmen Pedestrian Street 东门步行街
Shenzhen's oldest commercial street — a long pedestrian zone of clothing shops, street food stalls, and local restaurants. Budget-friendly and full of local energy, especially evenings and weekends. Good for a cheap lunch or people-watching.
Free Open 24h
Gangxia North Metro Station 岗厦北站
One of the most architecturally striking metro stations in China — and the world. A cavernous underground interchange designed as a vast inverted cone of light. Worth riding through just to see it. Located on Lines 9 and 14 in central Futian.
Metro fare Operates 6:00–23:00
🚇
Gangxia North Station — one of the most spectacular metro interiors in China
Dapeng Peninsula 大鹏半岛 Beaches · Ancient Fortress · Hiking

An hour east of the city centre, Dapeng feels like a different world entirely: sandy beaches, forested coastal hills, and a remarkably intact Ming dynasty fortress. It requires more effort to reach than central Shenzhen, but offers an experience unlike anything else in the Pearl River Delta. Go on a weekday to avoid weekend crowds from Hong Kong.

Dapeng Ancient Fortress 大鹏所城
A Ming dynasty (1394) walled garrison town in near-original condition — stone gates, ancestral halls, temples, and narrow alleys that feel genuinely removed from the 21st century. One of the most underrated heritage sites in Guangdong.
¥20 Open daily
Dameisha Beach 大梅沙海滨公园
Shenzhen's most accessible beach — clean, well-maintained, with calm water. Packed on summer weekends; far more relaxed on weekdays. A good combination with Dapeng Fortress as a full-day eastern trip.
Free Open daily
Wutong Mountain 梧桐山
The highest peak in Shenzhen at 943 m. Well-marked forested trails lead to the summit, which offers sweeping coastal views on clear days. Full ascent takes 2.5–3 hrs. Bring water; the trailhead is near Wutong Mountain Scenic Area bus stop.
Free Open daily
Jiaochangwei Beach 较场尾海滩
A quieter alternative to Dameisha — a long bay of golden sand lined with small guesthouses, seafood restaurants, and surf schools. Less commercialised and more local in feel. Popular with younger crowds and surfers in autumn and winter when the waves are best.
Free Open daily
🏖️
Jiaochangwei Beach — a quieter, more local bay on the Dapeng Peninsula

Dafen Oil Painting Village 大芬油画村 Art · Unique to Shenzhen

Dafen is one of Shenzhen's most curious and compelling places — a village-turned-global-art-production-hub where thousands of painters produce hand-painted reproductions of Western masterpieces. It once supplied an estimated 60% of the world's decorative oil paintings. Today it also hosts original artists' studios and a growing contemporary scene.

Gallery Lane Walking Route 大芬村
Wander through narrow alleyways lined with open studios — painters working live on Van Goghs, Monets, and Klimts, alongside original work. You can buy a reproduction from ¥50 or commission a custom portrait (¥200–500, ready in 1–2 days from a photo).
Free to browse 10:00–20:00
Dafen Art Museum 大芬美术馆
Rotating exhibitions by Dafen-based artists focused on contemporary and original work rather than reproductions. A good counterpoint to the commercial street outside.
Free Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00
Commission a portrait from a photo — most Dafen painters can complete a canvas in 1–2 days and ship internationally. Prices start around ¥200–300 for a single portrait. Arrive in the morning when painters are most willing to discuss custom work.

Getting There

By air, rail, or from Hong Kong

Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport (SZX)

Located in Bao'an district, ~30 km west of Futian CBD. Direct international routes to Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and some European hubs — most long-haul travellers connect via Hong Kong or Guangzhou.

  • Metro Line 11 (Airport Express) — fastest option; ~40 min to Futian¥20–30 · departs every 6–10 min · runs approx. 6:00–midnight
  • Taxi / DiDi — door to door; convenient for heavy luggage¥90–130 to Futian · 40–60 min · metered, no haggling
  • Airport shuttle bus — multiple lines covering districts not on Line 11¥15–25 · slower but useful for Nanshan and Shekou

From Hong Kong

Shenzhen is uniquely accessible from Hong Kong — three land border crossings and one sea route. All require a valid China visa (or applicable visa-free access).

Border queues at Luohu can reach 1–2 hours on weekends and public holidays. Futian crossing is generally faster. Avoid crossing during Chinese New Year and Golden Week if possible.

Getting Around

One of China's most extensive metro networks

Shenzhen's metro covers 18 lines and 199+ stations, connecting almost every major attraction. Fares are ¥2–9 depending on distance. Tap in and out with Alipay or WeChat Pay — no need for a separate card. Trains run approximately 6am–midnight.

DiDiDapeng, beach, anywhere off-metro¥15–80English-friendly app. Set up before arrival. Most reliable option.
TaxiShort hops, luggage, late nightFrom ¥10Green taxis: within SEZ only. Red: anywhere. Metered, no tipping.

Stay near an interchange station on Line 1 (Luobao Line) or Line 2 (Shekou Line) — these connect Luohu, Futian, Nanshan, and Shekou and cover the vast majority of tourist attractions.

Day Trips

Easy escapes within two hours of Shenzhen

Guangzhou

🚄 ~30 min by HSR

China's Cantonese food capital. Dim sum, the Canton Tower, Shamian Island colonial quarter, and Xiguan's traditional shophouse streets. An easy half or full day.

Hong Kong

🚶 ~15 min across border

A completely different urban world 15 minutes away. Victoria Peak, Kowloon street markets, and the MTR to anywhere. Requires a valid Hong Kong travel document.

Zhuhai & Macau

⛴️ ~1.5 hrs by ferry from Shekou

Ferry from Shekou to Zhuhai, then walk across to Macau. Portuguese architecture, egg tarts, the Historic Centre UNESCO site, and casinos if inclined.

Kaiping Diaolou

🚄 ~2 hrs HSR + bus

UNESCO-listed watchtower villages built by Cantonese emigrants in the early 20th century — a surreal fusion of Chinese and Italian Baroque architecture in the rice fields.

Practical Info

Everything you need to know before you go

CurrencyChinese Yuan / RMB (¥). Alipay and WeChat Pay accepted almost universally. ATMs in malls and metro stations accept foreign cards.
LanguageMandarin official; Cantonese widely spoken among locals. English is more common in Shekou, Nanshan, and Futian CBD than most mainland cities. Metro signage is bilingual.
Internet & VPNGoogle, WhatsApp, Instagram, and most Western apps are blocked. Install a VPN before you arrive — downloading one inside China is much harder. For navigation: Amap or Apple Maps. For messaging: WeChat.
VisaMost nationalities need a standard China visa. 144-hour visa-free transit is available for 54+ passport types through Bao'an Airport or Shekou sea port. See our Visa Guide.
WeatherSubtropical. Oct–Dec is best: sunny, cool, low humidity (20–26°C). Jun–Sep is hot and very humid (30–35°C) with typhoon risk. Always carry a compact umbrella.
TippingNot expected or customary. Rounding up DiDi fares is fine but not required. High-end hotel and restaurant service charges are usually included in the bill.
SafetyShenzhen is very safe for tourists. Standard urban precautions apply — be aware of pickpockets in Dongmen and at busy border crossings. Emergency: 110 (police), 120 (ambulance).
MedicalShenzhen has good private hospitals with English-speaking doctors. Recommended: Shenzhen International General Hospital (Futian), Boai Hospital. See our Medical Guide.
PaymentsSet up Alipay before arrival — foreigners can now link international Visa/Mastercard. This is the most important practical step for a smooth trip. See our Money Guide.
During Golden Week (Oct 1–7) and Chinese New Year, Shenzhen receives enormous visitor numbers from Hong Kong and across China. Border queues can reach 2–3 hours. Book accommodation months in advance.
